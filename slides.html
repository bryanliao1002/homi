02 · The problemhomi.

Shared space.
Different definitions of “fair.”

Dirty dishes, guest noise, uneven chores, and shared bills are small issues with a repeated human cost.

The same roommates can want a clean kitchen and disagree about when to wash up. A reminder can solve tonight without creating a rule everyone understands tomorrow.

“Later” might mean after dinner, after work, or before bed.
A request can sound like blame; silence can be mistaken for agreement.

Different standards

What counts as clean, quiet, or done?

Different schedules

When can each person act on a task?

Coordination split

Chats, lists, balances, and verbal norms live apart.

Social friction

Repeated reminders can make one roommate the enforcer.

Problem framing is qualitative; no prevalence or time-saved figure is claimed.02 / 10

What worked
Participants appreciated balanced treatment of both perspectives.

What to improve
One wanted shorter mediation; feedback also called for personalization and clearer next steps.

Fictional roommate scenarios; self-reported feedback; no controlled comparison. Real-world conflict reduction was not measured.

Demo profile switching is not authentication; current safety screening is keyword-based. Automated results do not establish real-world outcomes.
